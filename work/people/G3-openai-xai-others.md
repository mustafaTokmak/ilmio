- Plugin guidelines (https://developers.openai.com/plugins/plugin-guidelines.md , `openai-plugin-guidelines.md`), the lines that bear on an offer connector:
  - "Plugins must not serve advertisements and must not exist primarily as an advertising vehicle. Every plugin must deliver clear, legitimate functionality that provides standalone value to users."
  - "**Unofficial connectors:** We cannot approve plugins that primarily function as unofficial connectors to third-party services, including pass-through intermediary software layers."
  - "**Subtitles and descriptions:** ... Do not advertise pricing, subscriptions, free trials, discounts, or promotions."
  - "Currently, plugins may conduct commerce only for physical goods."
  - "Plugins should use external checkout, directing users to complete purchases on your own domain."
  - "Plugins must not apply ChatGPT-specific fees, surcharges, or other pricing that penalizes users for accessing a service through ChatGPT. Temporary discounts and promotional offers on other platforms are permitted."
  - "Avoid misleading, overly promotional, or comparative language (for example, `pick_me`, `best`, `official`)." (tool names)
  - "Plugins that demonstrate strong real-world utility and high user satisfaction may be eligible for enhanced distribution opportunities, such as directory placement or proactive suggestions."
- **[I]** Reading: a per-merchant plugin published by the merchant fits the text. A cross-store offer broker run by a third party collides with "unofficial connectors ... pass-through intermediary" and risks "advertising vehicle". The text does not say whether a seller's own concrete offer is an advertisement.
